## 1. THE FRUSTRATION: THE "HOMOGENEOUS AI" WALL

It started with a glaring industry problem: **AI models make decent websites, but they make the exact same website every single time.**

Despite having access to modern frontend libraries, every AI-assisted developer was stuck in the same visual trap:
- Dark-mode obsidian cards with purple/violet glowing borders.
- Static, disjointed SaaS rows of features that felt dead on arrival.
- Floaty, laggy custom cursors that fought native browser inputs.

### The Failed Naive Attempts:
To break out of this, conventional techniques were attempted:
- Taking screenshots of Awwwards, FWA, and CSSDA Site-of-the-Day winners.
- Screen-recording smooth interactions and feeding video frames to LLMs.
- Prompting models with explicit library names: *"Use GSAP ScrollTrigger! Use Three.js! Use Lenis!"*

**The Result**: Complete failure. The models named the tools, but they did not understand the **computational physics, input-to-computation pipelines, or mathematical invariants** required to make a digital canvas feel alive.

## 2. THE EPIPHANY: UNIVERSITY EXPERT SYSTEMS & KNOWLEDGE EXTRACTION

The breakthrough came from remembering foundational computer science coursework on **Classical Expert Systems and Knowledge Engineering**.

The realization was profound:
> **Real expertise is not naming libraries. Expertise is structured procedural knowledge, elicitation probes, and inference models extracted from ground truth.**

### Ingesting the PhD Literature:
Instead of relying on base LLM training data, seminal PhD-level Knowledge Engineering literature was hunted down:
- *Knowledge Engineering and Management (KEM)*
- *Rule-Based Expert Systems: The MYCIN Experiments of the Stanford Heuristic Programming Project* (Buchanan & Shortliffe, 1984; 752 pages).

Because the seminal MYCIN book was split across 45 fragmented chapter PDFs on legacy servers, an automated, idempotent acquisition, sequential collation, and 1-bit monochrome Lanczos compression pipeline was engineered (`download_mycin_pdfs.ps1`, `merge_mycin_pdfs.py`, `compress_mycin_pdf.py`), crushing 37MB of noisy scans to 21MB of razor-sharp typography.

From these foundational texts, two core parent skills were compiled:
1. **`source-to-skill`**: A domain-agnostic meta-compiler utilizing CommonKADS elicitation probes (**P1–P6**: Conditions, Prerequisites, Exceptions, Alternatives, Invariants, Checks) capable of extracting procedural expertise from any unstructured source.
2. **`expert-system-engineer`**: A formal rule-based inference and certainty-factor architecture.
